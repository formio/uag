# College application portal

A three-persona admissions workflow (student, college administrator, scholarship admin). Exercises a public onboarding wizard with email verification, a multi-stage status pipeline driven by two review forms, a calculated score with threshold-based outcomes, status-filtered visibility per role, and email notifications at three points in the workflow. The heaviest workflow example — the data model is simple, but the state machine and cross-role handoffs are not.

## Prompt

```
/formio-application Build a college application portal for our admissions office. Three kinds of people use it. Prospective students arrive unauthenticated and go through an onboarding walkthrough that collects their account info, then get a verification email; once verified they fill out the college application itself, and afterward they can come back to see the status of their application and any updates. College administrators log in and see a data table of every applicant. They evaluate each application by filling out an assessment that scores seven criteria from 1 to 10 — academics, testing, extracurricular & impact, leadership & initiative, service orientation, essays, and recommendations — with a notes field for each criterion. The seven scores average into an overall applicant score: 5 and below is declined, 6 to 8 is accepted without scholarship, 9 to 10 is accepted with scholarship. The student gets notified when their assessment is submitted, and if they were accepted with scholarship the scholarship admin gets notified to evaluate the award. Scholarship admins log in and see ONLY the applicants who were accepted with scholarship; those applicants sit in either a "scholarship evaluation" or "scholarship granted" state so the admin can track who still needs an award. They review the application along with the administrator's assessment and notes, then pick the award level: Full Ride (exceptional), Paid Room & Board plus 25% discount (great), Supplemental scholarship at 25% discount (good), or a single $1000 reward (average). Once they decide, the student is notified and can see their scholarship reward in the portal. Put the app in ~/Documents/formio/apps/college-application.
```

## What to look for

- Three roles beyond anonymous (student, college administrator, scholarship admin) plus a status pipeline on the Application resource — something like submitted → assessed (declined / accepted / accepted with scholarship) → scholarship evaluation → scholarship granted. The planner should ask whether status lives on the Application or is derived from the Assessment/Award records rather than guess.
- Onboarding as a registration path (wizard or form) feeding the user resource, with the email-verification requirement surfacing as an email concern — the planner owns the registration form and Role Assignment; anything beyond that (verification-token mechanics) should route toward `formio-auth`, not get invented inline.
- Assessment as its own Resource referencing the Application, with seven 1–10 score fields each paired with a notes field, and the overall score as a calculated average — not seven disconnected forms and not a hand-entered total.
- The 5/6–8/9–10 thresholds should drive the outcome (declined / accepted / accepted with scholarship) as calculated or conditional logic, not be left for the administrator to select by hand.
- Access separation: students see only their own application and its status (`own`-scoped), administrators see all applications, scholarship admins see only accepted-with-scholarship applicants — the last one is a status-conditioned view, and the planner should be explicit about whether that's enforced server-side (submission access / filtered queries) or is front-end filtering.
- Scholarship Award (or equivalent) as a Resource referencing the Application, with the four award levels as a select; the scholarship admin's view must surface the administrator's assessment scores and notes read-only.
- Three Email Actions in the plan: verification at registration, student notification on assessment submit, scholarship-admin notification on the accepted-with-scholarship outcome — plus the final award notification to the student.
- Multi-role assignment done right: a single `user` resource with a `role` selectboxes component (one value per persona) and one CONDITIONAL Role Assignment action per persona on the user resource — not just the register form's unconditional student assignment, and never a plan that assumes staff roles can be set by writing the submission `roles` array directly (the API strips those writes, even for the project owner).
